<H3 div align="center"> Features </H3>
It's a completely free, private and open source app with 0 in-app purchases, not now not ever I don't believe such a helpful app should cost money nor annoy about optionally buying the app, you can:

- Use it to track your money manually or be used as an expense tracker
- Set up budget or goals for yourself
- See a broad overview of your inputted financial data, budget status, upcoming sub/recurring payments and etc
- Restyle the whole app with built-in themes (Classic, High Contrast, and a flat Material You theme on Android and the web), your own JSON themes, and per-device overrides for the text font, number font, app icons and category icons
- Export & Import transaction CSVs, and import data from other apps - Currently just supports from Cashew, it's just the app I previously used...shoutout to bro his app is fine overall

<H3 div align="center"> Import & export </H3>
**More → Import & export** (`/csv`) exports and imports transactions as CSV, and can **import from other apps**. The first supported app is Cashew: its backup file (an `.sql` file that is really a SQLite database) brings over wallets, categories and subcategories, tags, transactions, transfers, recurring schedules and budgets, and every account's balance is checked against the backup before anything is saved. Loans and debts, objectives, income budgets (saving targets), shared and filtered budgets, app settings and scanner templates are not imported, and imported schedules wait for your review instead of posting automatically. For each active schedule, Cashew's next unpaid entry comes in as an upcoming transaction linked to it (later unpaid entries are generated by the schedule as they fall due). Cashew pays subscriptions itself when it is opened, so an overdue entry is kept as a live schedule unless Cashew's own auto-pay setting is off or it has been overdue for a very long time. Budget category limits that Cashew keeps as percentages (its default) are converted into amounts of the budget limit. The file is read entirely on the device with no network use. You preview the result first and can either add to your data or permanently delete your current data and import the backup in its place. That delete is real (the rows are erased, not hidden) and cannot be undone, so it is only available on a device that does not sync: your other devices would keep the data and send it back.

<H3 div align="center"> Deleting </H3>
Deleting something erases it. The name, amount, date, note, links and every other detail you entered are wiped from the device, not hidden. What remains is a small marker: the record's id, when it was deleted, and its kind. The marker is kept so your other devices learn about the delete instead of syncing the record back, and so a deleted recurring payment is not generated again. On iOS and Android the database also overwrites freed space. With sync on, older encrypted sync history that still holds the content is dropped once all your devices have caught up, or after 90 days at most.

<H3 div align="center"> Sync </H3>
Qashy can sync between your own devices. It is **off until you turn it on**, there is no account, and it is
end-to-end encrypted with keys that never leave your devices.

- Devices are paired in person by scanning a QR code and confirming a 6-word code shown on both screens.
- On the same network, devices connect directly and contact no server at all.
- When they can't, sealed and padded ciphertext is left in a blind drop-box for the other device to collect.
  The server sees an opaque identifier, ciphertext, and an IP address — never who you are, what changed, or
  how much of it there is. The app ships pointed at the project's own relay; swap or blank it under
  **More → Sync → Advanced**.
- Conflicting edits merge automatically without losing either side; deletions and rejected updates are
  recorded in a visible activity log rather than applied silently.

Read [docs/sync-threat-model.md](docs/sync-threat-model.md) for the full model, including a plain list of
what sync deliberately does **not** protect against.

Sync uses WebRTC through a native module, so **Expo Go cannot run it** — use a development build.

<H3 div align="center"> Exchange rates </H3>
Accounts in another currency can convert automatically. It is the app's second deliberate network
exception, and it is **on by default** but can be turned off per device.

- While enabled, Qashy asks [frankfurter.dev](https://frankfurter.dev) for a rate only when something needs one — entering a foreign currency, a recurring schedule coming due, a foreign account — pivoted through
  EUR for precision. Only currency codes and a date ever leave the device — never an amount, an account name,
  or anything else. Frankfurter has no key and needs no account, but it can see the device's IP address.
- The on/off flag lives in local sync metadata, not in the settings that sync replicates to your other
  devices — turning it off is a per-device choice, not a vault-wide one.
- Turned off, or for a currency Frankfurter doesn't cover, exchange rates fall back to the manual rate you
  enter yourself under **More → Exchange rates**.

<H3 div align="center"> Themes </H3>
Themes are pure data and bundled assets: nothing is downloaded for a theme. The **Material You** theme is flat (tonal surfaces and outlines instead of shadows and gradients) and on Android 12 and newer takes its colors from the wallpaper through a small local native module (`modules/qashy-dynamic-colors`); it needs a development build or release build, not Expo Go. iOS offers Classic and High Contrast; imported custom themes work on every platform. **More → Appearance** also lets each device override the text font, the numbers font, the app icon set and the category icon set; those four overrides are stored per device and never synced. Custom themes are documented in [CUSTOM_THEME_GUIDE.md](CUSTOM_THEME_GUIDE.md). Bundled fonts and icon art and their licenses are listed in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

**Feel.** Every amount field on a phone opens a calculator keypad in place of the system keyboard (it is not persistent: it appears when you tap the amount and goes with Done or when you move to a text field; the exchange-rate and percentage fields are not money and keep the number keyboard), and typed expressions like `12+3.5` are evaluated everywhere. On a phone-width Transactions screen, scrolling down folds the search and type filters away and shrinks the Income / Spent / Net tiles to one line; scrolling up brings them back. Press, hold and drag across transaction rows to select a range (it scrolls at the list edges); selecting folds the top of the screen away the same way scrolling does, and a compact bar offers **Edit** (change the category or date of every selected transaction) and **Delete**. Every date is chosen from a calendar (the system picker on iOS and Android, the browser's date control on the web) rather than typed, and a recurring schedule can end on a date or after a number of times, for installment plans. Categories are picked from a single sideways-scrolling row in the transaction and recurring forms. The calculator keypad slides up from the bottom and closes when you tap outside the field, and remembers which category you last gave a title, suggesting it until you pick one yourself. After saving, the new row scrolls into view and flashes briefly; future-dated transactions sit in a collapsible Upcoming group. Tapping the active section (tab, rail or sidebar) scrolls it to the top. On the web every visited section stays mounted, so scroll position and search survive a switch, and sections cross-fade. **More → Appearance → Animations** (All, Minimal, Off; per device, never synced) scales or removes entrances, exits, layout transitions and count-up numbers, on top of the system reduced-motion setting.

<H3 div align="center"> No patched libraries </H3>
Qashy does not modify any third-party package: there is no `patch-package`, no `postinstall` script and no `patches/` folder, so an Expo SDK upgrade can never silently drop a local edit. The two Android behaviors that used to need a patch to `react-native-screens` are built on public APIs instead:

- **Long-pressing the native bottom bar** opens the "Navigation bar style" sheet (native or floating; also reachable from Appearance). The local Expo module `modules/qashy-tab-gestures` finds Android's `BottomNavigationView` in the activity's view hierarchy, takes over each item's long-click listener (which also suppresses Material's tooltip) and emits an `onTabLongPress` event that `useTabLongPress` hands to the navigation layout. It is a no-op on iOS, web, Expo Go and Jest.
- **The Android creation/editing sheets** (`transaction`, `budget`, `goal`, `account` and the rest) are drawn by `AndroidSheet` (`src/components/navigation/android-sheet.tsx`): a transparent-modal route with no native transition, mounted by the root stack's `screenLayout`. Its spring entrance, accelerating exit, grabber, two heights (72% and full, dragged between), swipe-to-close, keyboard avoidance and unsaved-changes confirmation are all Reanimated, gesture-handler and React Native. iOS and the web keep the native form sheet. Deliberately not carried over from Android's native sheet: edge-to-edge system insets beyond the navigation bar, and Material's own `BottomSheetBehavior` nested-scroll handling. Both native modules need a development or release build, not Expo Go.

If a future `react-native-screens` makes the bar something other than a Material `BottomNavigationView`, long-press simply stops working; nothing else breaks.
