<H3 div align="center"> Features </H3>
It's a completely free, private and open source app with 0 in-app purchases, not now not ever I don't believe such a helpful app should cost money nor annoy about optionally buying the app, you can:

- Use it to track your money manually or be used as an expense tracker
- Set up budget or goals for yourself
- See a broad overview of your inputted financial data, budget status, upcoming sub/recurring payments and etc
- Restyle the whole app with built-in themes (Classic, High Contrast, and a flat Material You theme on Android and the web), your own JSON themes, and per-device overrides for the text font, number font, app icons and category icons
- Export & Import transaction CSVs, and import data from other apps - Currently just supports from Cashew, it's just the app I previously used...shoutout to bro his app is fine overall
- Put a Quick add (a one-tap add button with this month's net worth, spent and income) or a Budget (progress of one budget) widget on the Android home screen, styled per widget and hidden-amounts aware (see Widgets below)

<H3 div align="center"> Import & export </H3>
**More → Import & export** (`/csv`) exports and imports transactions as CSV, and can **import from other apps**. The first supported app is Cashew: its backup file (an `.sql` file that is really a SQLite database) brings over wallets, categories and subcategories, tags, transactions, transfers, recurring schedules and budgets, and every account's balance is checked against the backup before anything is saved. Accounts, categories and budgets archived in Cashew are not imported (a transfer with an archived account comes in as income or expense on the other account, and an archived category's transactions come in uncategorized). Loans and debts, objectives, income budgets (saving targets), shared and filtered budgets, app settings and scanner templates are not imported, and imported schedules wait for your review instead of posting automatically. For each active schedule, Cashew's next unpaid entry comes in as an upcoming transaction linked to it (later unpaid entries are generated by the schedule as they fall due). Cashew pays subscriptions itself when it is opened, so an overdue entry is kept as a live schedule unless Cashew's own auto-pay setting is off or it has been overdue for a very long time. Budget category limits that Cashew keeps as percentages (its default) are converted into amounts of the budget limit. The file is read entirely on the device with no network use. You preview the result first and can either add to your data or permanently delete your current data and import the backup in its place. That delete is real (the rows are erased, not hidden) and cannot be undone, so it is only available on a device that does not sync: your other devices would keep the data and send it back.

<H3 div align="center"> Deleting </H3>
Deleting something erases it, and nothing is archived. Deleting an account deletes the transactions and recurring schedules on it too; a transfer between it and another account is kept on that other account, as income or expense. The confirmation says exactly how many transactions and schedules go and which accounts receive such a transfer. A budget that loses its last account, category or tag filter is kept and then covers all spending. Data an earlier version archived is deleted the next time the app starts. The name, amount, date, note, links and every other detail you entered are wiped from the device, not hidden. What remains is a small marker: the record's id, when it was deleted, and its kind. The marker is kept so your other devices learn about the delete instead of syncing the record back, and so a deleted recurring payment is not generated again. On Android the database also overwrites freed space. With sync on, older encrypted sync history that still holds the content is dropped once all your devices have caught up, or after 90 days at most.

<H3 div align="center"> Sync </H3>
Qashy can sync between your own devices. It is **off until you turn it on**, there is no account, and it is
end-to-end encrypted with keys that never leave your devices.

- Devices are paired in person by scanning a QR code and confirming a 6-word code shown on both screens.
- Sealed and padded ciphertext is left in a blind drop-box for the other device to collect.
  The server sees an opaque identifier, ciphertext, and an IP address — never who you are, what changed, or
  how much of it there is. Routing tags rotate every UTC day, so the server cannot link a device's traffic
  across days. The app ships pointed at the project's own relay; swap or blank it under
  **More → Sync → Advanced**. With no relay, devices sync only through transfer files you carry
  between them yourself.
- Conflicting edits merge automatically without losing either side; deletions and rejected updates are
  recorded in a visible activity log rather than applied silently.
- Sync normally runs only when you open the app. On Android you can opt in, per device and off by default,
  to **More → Sync → Sync in the background**: about every 30 minutes, when the device is connected and
  the battery is not low, Qashy checks for changes from your other devices (so widgets stay current) and
  applies them. It uses some battery and data, and **the relay can see when this device checks in**. It never
  posts recurring transactions, never runs while the app is open, and the switch is stored only on this
  device. It needs the device to be unlocked enough for Android to release the sync key; otherwise that check
  is skipped.

Read [docs/sync-threat-model.md](docs/sync-threat-model.md) for the full model, including a plain list of
what sync deliberately does **not** protect against.

<H3 div align="center"> Widgets </H3>
On Android, Qashy offers two home-screen widgets (long-press the home screen, choose Widgets, then Qashy), each with a preview in the picker: **Quick add** (a rounded add button that opens a new transaction, beside this month's net worth, spent and income; at one or two cells wide it is just the button, and you can switch each figure off) and **Budget** (one budget's progress, as a bar when short and a ring when taller, with the percent used, what is left or over, and the pace; it follows the budget you pick, or the first active one). Tapping the figures opens Overview and tapping the budget opens Plan. **More → Widgets** (`/widgets`; Android only) sets, per placed widget, the theme (Qashy or Material You), mode (system, light or dark), background (theme surface, accent tint or a custom `#RRGGBB` color), transparency, a frosted look (a soft imitation: a widget cannot blur what is behind it), which figures show or which budget, plus one **Hide amounts** switch that masks every amount on all widgets (the Budget widget then shows only its percent and pace). Android 12 and newer also open this screen from the widget's long-press **Reconfigure**.

Widgets are a local feature with no network use and no sync:

- A widget cannot read the app's database, so the app keeps a **formatted copy of exactly what the widgets show** (display strings, not records) in the app's private, no-backup storage (`modules/qashy-widgets`). It is excluded from Android backups and device transfers, is replaced whenever the shown data changes, and is deleted when the last widget is removed or after a "Delete my current data and import" replace import. It is plain text on the device, inside the same app sandbox as the database; **Hide amounts** keeps amounts out of it entirely.
- Widgets refresh as soon as the data they show, their settings or a custom theme changes while the app is open (and again when you leave the app or return to it), at midnight (so the month and the day's figures stay right), and when the system time, timezone or language changes. They never post recurring transactions: a schedule that comes due still waits for the app to post it, but the widgets already count the auto-post occurrences that are due, as a read-only preview that stores nothing. Changes from your other devices reach the widgets when you open the app, unless you turn on the optional **Sync in the background** switch (see Sync below).
- Widget settings (including Hide amounts) are device-local in the sync metadata table, never in the synced settings, and are never sent to your other devices.

<H3 div align="center"> Exchange rates </H3>
Accounts in another currency can convert automatically. It is the app's second deliberate network
exception, and it is **on by default** but can be turned off per device.

- While enabled, Qashy asks [frankfurter.dev](https://frankfurter.dev) for a rate only when something needs one — entering a foreign currency, a recurring schedule coming due, a foreign account — pivoted through
  EUR for precision. Only currency codes and a date ever leave the device — never an amount, an account name,
  or anything else. Frankfurter has no key and needs no account, but it can see the device's IP address.
- The on/off flag lives in local sync metadata, not in the settings that sync replicates to your other
  devices — turning it off is a per-device choice, not a vault-wide one.
- Turned off, or for a currency Frankfurter doesn't cover, exchange rates fall back to the manual rate you
  enter yourself under **More → Exchange rates**.

<H3 div align="center"> Themes </H3>
Themes are pure data and bundled assets: nothing is downloaded for a theme. The **Material You** theme is flat (tonal surfaces and outlines instead of shadows and gradients) and on Android 12 and newer takes its colors from the wallpaper through a small local native module (`modules/qashy-dynamic-colors`); it needs a development build or release build, not Expo Go. Imported custom themes work on every platform. **More → Appearance** also lets each device override the text font, the numbers font, the app icon set and the category icon set; those four overrides are stored per device and never synced. Custom themes are documented in [CUSTOM_THEME_GUIDE.md](CUSTOM_THEME_GUIDE.md). Bundled fonts and icon art and their licenses are listed in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

**Language.** The app language follows the device: the first device language Qashy supports (English or Hebrew) is used, otherwise English, and every number, amount and date is formatted with that language. **More → Language** sets a per-device choice instead. It is never stored in the synced settings and never leaves the device. On Android it is the system per-app language (`modules/qashy-locale`), so the same choice appears in Android Settings → Languages → App languages; on the web it is stored in this browser's device-local metadata. Supported languages are listed in `src/localization/languages.json`.

**Feel.** Every amount field on a phone opens a calculator keypad in place of the system keyboard (it is not persistent: it appears when you tap the amount and goes with Done or when you move to a text field; the exchange-rate and percentage fields are not money and keep the number keyboard), and typed expressions like `12+3.5` are evaluated everywhere. On a phone-width Transactions screen, scrolling down folds the search and type filters away and shrinks the Income / Spent / Net tiles to one line; scrolling up brings them back. Press, hold and drag across transaction rows to select a range (it scrolls at the list edges); selecting folds the top of the screen away the same way scrolling does, and a compact bar offers **Edit** (change the category or date of every selected transaction) and **Delete**. Every date is chosen from a calendar (the system picker on Android, the browser's date control on the web) rather than typed, and a recurring schedule can end on a date or after a number of times, for installment plans. Categories are picked from a single sideways-scrolling row in the transaction and recurring forms. The calculator keypad slides up from the bottom and closes when you tap outside the field, and remembers which category you last gave a title, suggesting it until you pick one yourself. After saving, the new row scrolls into view and flashes briefly; future-dated transactions sit in a collapsible Upcoming group. Tapping the active section (tab, rail or sidebar) scrolls it to the top. On the web every visited section stays mounted, so scroll position and search survive a switch, and sections cross-fade. **More → Appearance → Animations** (All, Minimal, Off; per device, never synced) scales or removes entrances, exits, layout transitions and count-up numbers, on top of the system reduced-motion setting.

<H3 div align="center"> Adding a Play-translated language </H3>
English and Hebrew are bundled. Any other language is translated by Google Play and shown on Android only (the web app stays English and Hebrew). The Android build writes the English UI strings as resources (`values/strings.xml`, from `src/localization/android-strings.json`), Play translates them, and the app reads the translations of the chosen language at startup with one `QashyLocale.getStrings` call. Anything Play has not translated stays English.

1. In Play Console → Translations → App strings, select the target languages. **Never select English or Hebrew**: Play overwrites the existing translations of every language you select, and Hebrew is translated by hand in `src/localization/messages/he.ts`.
2. Add `{ "tag": "fr-FR", "label": "Français" }` to `playTranslated` in `src/localization/languages.json`. The label is the language's own name, shown as is in every UI language.
3. After changing `HEBREW` or the dynamic patterns in `src/localization/messages/he.ts`, run `npm run strings:android`. It rewrites `android-strings.json` (the catalog Play translates) and `android-runtime.json` (the small list of resource names and pattern sources the app imports at runtime), and a test fails when they are out of date. The generator runs TypeScript directly through Node's type stripping, so it needs Node 22.18 or newer.

Strings without a `HEBREW` entry are not in the catalog and stay English. Roughly a seventh of the dynamic patterns in `HEBREW`'s table are skipped and stay English in Play languages: most translate a captured fragment (a field label, a relative time or a list of category names, which a resource template cannot express), and a few are not plain text with capture groups (optional or non-capturing parts, or a quantifier on a literal). `npm run strings:android` prints the exact counts and each skipped pattern with its reason. A skipped pattern still claims the messages it matches, so a broader pattern can never translate them instead.

`src/localization/__tests__/untranslated-literals.test.ts` keeps the dictionary complete: it fails when a string a user can see (UI text, `t()` calls, error messages thrown by the repository, the import readers, sync and the theme validator, import warnings, and parameter defaults) has no Hebrew entry or pattern, when text on a `literal` element would show untranslated, or when a broad pattern would let English words through. Exceptions are listed in the test with a reason each.

<H3 div align="center"> No patched libraries </H3>
Qashy does not modify any third-party package: there is no `patch-package`, no `postinstall` script and no `patches/` folder, so an Expo SDK upgrade can never silently drop a local edit. The two Android behaviors that used to need a patch to `react-native-screens` are built on public APIs instead:

- **Long-pressing the native bottom bar** opens the "Navigation bar style" sheet (native or floating; also reachable from Appearance). The local Expo module `modules/qashy-tab-gestures` finds Android's `BottomNavigationView` in the activity's view hierarchy, takes over each item's long-click listener (which also suppresses Material's tooltip) and emits an `onTabLongPress` event that `useTabLongPress` hands to the navigation layout. It is a no-op on web, Expo Go and Jest.
- **The Android creation/editing sheets** (`transaction`, `budget`, `goal`, `account` and the rest) are drawn by `AndroidSheet` (`src/components/navigation/android-sheet.tsx`): a transparent-modal route with no native transition, mounted by the root stack's `screenLayout`. Its spring entrance, accelerating exit, grabber, two heights (72% and full, dragged between), swipe-to-close, keyboard avoidance and unsaved-changes confirmation are all Reanimated, gesture-handler and React Native. The web keeps the form sheet. Deliberately not carried over from Android's native sheet: edge-to-edge system insets beyond the navigation bar, and Material's own `BottomSheetBehavior` nested-scroll handling. Both native modules need a development or release build, not Expo Go.

If a future `react-native-screens` makes the bar something other than a Material `BottomNavigationView`, long-press simply stops working; nothing else breaks.
